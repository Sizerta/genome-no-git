A SNP is an edge

Eight haplotypes, 50,000 bp, simulated under the coalescent with recombination. Drag the position marker and watch the genealogy change. Every variant in your genotype matrix is a mutation sitting on one branch, carried by exactly the samples beneath it. That is the whole idea behind ancestral recombination graphs, and this page is the same data shown as a tree and as a matrix at the same time.

What to take from it

  1. One coordinate, one tree

    At any single base, the eight haplotypes have exactly one genealogy. Samples sit along the bottom, time runs upward in generations before the present. Coalescence times are heavily skewed, which is why the axis defaults to a square root. Switch it to linear and most of the structure piles up near zero. That skew is real coalescent behaviour, not a drawing artefact.

  2. A mutation lives on a branch

    Place a mutation on an edge and every sample below it inherits the derived allele. That carrier set is a column of the genotype matrix. Allele frequency is just the fraction of samples under the branch, so a mutation high in the tree is common and one near a leaf is a singleton. Hover any column on the right to see its branch on the left.

  3. Recombination edits the tree, it does not replace it

    Move the marker across a breakpoint and the topology changes, but only slightly. This genome has 7 local trees drawn from 20 nodes, and adjacent trees differ at only 4 to 5 nodes. Store the edits rather than the trees and a genealogy for half a million people fits in memory. That is what a tree sequence is, and why tskit exists.

  4. Linkage is tree similarity

    Two mutations on the same branch have identical carrier sets, so they are in perfect LD, at any physical distance. Two mutations in different local trees have carrier sets that no longer match. LD decay along a chromosome is the genealogy being rewritten as you walk. The correlation matrix you normally estimate from genotypes is a downstream summary of this.

  5. Relatedness is a local quantity

    Pick two samples and look at the bottom panel. Their time to a common ancestor jumps at every breakpoint. Average it along the genome and you have one entry of the relatedness matrix that goes into a mixed model. The genome-wide number hides the variation you can see here, and that variation is why self-relatedness differs between individuals.

Simulated with msprime: eight sample haplotypes, Ne = 10,000, 50 kb, recombination 2.5×10−9, mutation 5.8×10−9 per base per generation. 35 segregating sites, 35 edges. The topology is a genuine coalescent simulation, so the uneven branch lengths and the wandering TMRCA are what the model actually produces. Further reading: Nielsen, Vaughn & Deng, Nature Reviews Genetics 26:47–58 (2025).
